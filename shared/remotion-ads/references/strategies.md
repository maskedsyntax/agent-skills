# Strategy library

Read this **index**, score what fits, then open only the winning entries.

Do not default to FOMO.

| ID | Strategy | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fomo` | FOMO | The viewer is late to a visible, real shift |
| `urgency` | Urgency | A true time or capacity constraint exists |
| `aspirational` | Aspirational / Feel-good | The product is a better self, not a tool list |
| `pain-solution` | Pain → Solution | A sharp, familiar frustration exists |
| `transformation` | Transformation | Before/after is visible and honest |
| `curiosity` | Curiosity / Mystery | The mechanism is surprising |
| `contrarian` | Contrarian | The category has a lie everyone repeats |
| `product-first` | Product-first / Visual Desire | The UI or output is itself beautiful |
| `demo` | Demo / Watch This | One action is the whole sale |
| `speed` | Speed / Convenience | Time-to-result is the product |
| `social-proof` | Social Proof | Real proof exists (do not invent) |
| `objection-killer` | Objection Killer | One objection blocks purchase |
| `relatable` | Relatable / "That's Me" | The audience shares a specific scene |
| `story` | Story-driven | A 15s character beat is stronger than a feature |
| `challenge` | Challenge | A constraint or dare is native to the product |
| `feature-wow` | Feature Wow Moment | One interaction is visually shocking |
| `premium` | Premium / Status | Craft, taste, or exclusivity is real |
| `chaos-calm` | Chaos → Calm | The product produces order you can see |
| `loss-aversion` | Loss Aversion | They are already losing something (photos, time, money) |
| `identity` | Identity | The product is who they are, not what they use |

Score 1–10: product fit, audience fit, hook strength, visual potential, emotional strength, conversion potential, asset feasibility, originality. Average. Rank top 5. Explore top 3.

---

## fomo

**Psychological goal.** Make inaction feel like falling behind a real movement.

**Works when.** There is a genuine cultural or category shift the viewer can recognize (new format, new constraint, friends already doing it).

**Does not work.** Unknown product, no social reality, solo utility tools with no scene around them.

**Hooks.** "You are still ___"; a crowd of the new behavior; a dated version of the old way, framed as embarrassing.

**Visual language.** Escalating scale, more devices entering frame, faster inserts of the new behavior.

**Pacing.** Fast, rising. Do not start at 11 — arrive there.

**Copy.** Short, second person, present tense. No fake "everyone".

**Ideal for.** Social products, cultural tools, new creative formats.

**Ethics.** No invented adoption numbers, no fake friend activity, no false exclusivity.

**Failures.** Generic "don't miss out", stock-party footage, countdown timers with no event.

## urgency

**Psychological goal.** Compress decision time around a real deadline or limit.

**Works when.** A sale, launch window, seasonal job, or product-native limit is real (daily cap, event, shipping cutoff).

**Does not work.** Evergreen utility. Fake 23:59 timers.

**Hooks.** A clock that belongs to the product; "today's ___ is already going".

**Visual language.** Tight crops on counters, progress bars, filling slots.

**Pacing.** Tight. Silence, then a hit.

**Copy.** Concrete time. No "limited time" unless it is.

**Ideal for.** Events, daily-use apps with real caps, launches.

**Ethics.** The deadline must exist. No dark-pattern scarcity.

**Failures.** Red banners, spinning clocks, "BUY NOW" on a calm tool.

## aspirational

**Psychological goal.** Let the viewer feel the life on the other side of the product.

**Works when.** The outcome is emotional and visible: calmer mornings, better photos, a finished book, a clean inbox.

**Does not work.** Pure infrastructure, APIs, internal tools.

**Hooks.** The result, unnamed; light opening up; a body at ease.

**Visual language.** Slower camera, air, wider frames later, warm light. Product arrives as the instrument, not the hero at frame 0.

**Pacing.** Gentle, expanding.

**Copy.** Sparse. Almost none in the first seconds.

**Ideal for.** Lifestyle, health-adjacent (no medical claims), creative, journaling, photo.

**Ethics.** Do not imply health/finance outcomes you cannot support. No fake "after" bodies or bank balances.

**Failures.** Stock sunset + logo. Inspiration-poster type.

## pain-solution

**Psychological goal.** Name the wound precisely, then show the shortest relief.

**Works when.** The frustration is specific and visual (40-tab mess, a camera roll of near-duplicates, a 12-email thread).

**Does not work.** Vague "productivity pain".

**Hooks.** Extreme crop of the mess; the sound of the problem; the exact sentence they say out loud.

**Visual language.** Chaos first (handheld, tight, overlapping UI), then a clean geometric solution shot.

**Pacing.** 40–50% pain, then a decisive cut.

**Copy.** Their vocabulary, not brand vocabulary.

**Ideal for.** Utilities, consumer tools, B2B with a visible workflow.

**Ethics.** Do not exaggerate harm. Do not shame.

**Failures.** "Tired of X?" on a stock photo. Solution is a feature list.

## transformation

**Psychological goal.** Make the delta undeniable.

**Works when.** Before and after can be shown with real product output.

**Does not work.** When the after is a feeling you cannot photograph, or the after requires fake metrics.

**Hooks.** Smash cut from wreckage to result. Wipe, split, match-cut on the same object.

**Visual language.** Locked framing so the change is the only motion. Then a camera move once the after is earned.

**Pacing.** Hold before long enough to sting.

**Copy.** Almost none. The pictures argue.

**Ideal for.** Editors, generators, cleaners, converters, design tools.

**Ethics.** Same source asset. No makeup-ad fraud. No invented analytics.

**Failures.** Two unrelated screenshots. "Before / After" labels doing all the work.

---

## curiosity

**Psychological goal.** Open a loop the product can close.

**Works when.** The mechanism is non-obvious ("it only lets you take 12", "it hides the feed").

**Does not work.** Obvious CRUD apps.

**Hooks.** A withheld object; a wrong-looking UI; a rule announced without context.

**Visual language.** Darkness, partial reveals, macro details that do not yet parse.

**Pacing.** Hold the question. Answer by second 4–8, not 14.

**Copy.** Incomplete sentences. Questions only if the picture is also a question.

**Ideal for.** Novel constraints, unusual UX, surprising tech.

**Ethics.** Do not bait with a different product than you deliver. No fake "secret".

**Failures.** Clickbait that disconnects from the demo. Mystery that never pays off.

## contrarian

**Psychological goal.** Break a category commandment the viewer is tired of.

**Works when.** You can name the lie ("you do not need another feed", "AI should not write this for you") and the product lives that stance.

**Does not work.** When you are actually the same as competitors.

**Hooks.** The rejected idea, crossed out in picture, not in a strikethrough font overlay only.

**Visual language.** One quiet, confident frame against a noisy category look.

**Pacing.** Slow enough to feel sure.

**Copy.** Blunt. No hedging.

**Ideal for.** Opinionated indie apps, privacy tools, anti-feature products.

**Ethics.** Do not slander competitors with false claims. Attack a practice, not a person.

**Failures.** Edgy type on a generic UI. Contrarian headline, conventional demo.

## product-first

**Psychological goal.** Desire the object. The UI or output is the talent.

**Works when.** Screens, motion, or generated artifacts are genuinely beautiful.

**Does not work.** Ugly but useful enterprise screens — use demo or pain instead.

**Hooks.** Macro materiality: glass, type, a color grade sweeping a photo, a gesture.

**Visual language.** Fashion-film lighting. Device as sculpture. Full-bleed UI.

**Pacing.** Unhurried, tactile.

**Copy.** Minimal or none until CTA.

**Ideal for.** Photo, fashion, design, music, well-designed consumer apps.

**Ethics.** Use real UI or real output. No dribbble mock that the app is not.

**Failures.** Fake glassmorphism, endless gradients, spinning 3D phones with no UI.

## demo

**Psychological goal.** "I get it" in one watched action.

**Works when.** A single path from tap to result is the sale.

**Does not work.** Multi-role platforms that need a narrative.

**Hooks.** Cursor already moving; a finger mid-tap; the result mid-transform.

**Visual language.** Camera tracks the action. Macro on the control, then pull out to the result.

**Pacing.** Precise. No extra shots.

**Copy.** Verb + object. No adjectives.

**Ideal for.** Devtools, converters, editors, well-scoped utilities.

**Ethics.** Real sequence. Do not skip a step that is actually painful.

**Failures.** Hovering cursor with no purpose. Five features "demoed" in 15s.

---

## speed

**Psychological goal.** Make slowness feel expensive.

**Works when.** Time-to-value is dramatically short and visible.

**Does not work.** Slow but deep products (use transformation or premium).

**Hooks.** A stopwatch that is a joke (0.4s). A long way vs the short way.

**Visual language.** Whip cuts, motion blur, result slam. Then stillness on the outcome.

**Pacing.** Fast until the result, then hold.

**Copy.** Numbers only if verified. Otherwise show elapsed time in picture.

**Ideal for.** Automation, search, generators with real speed.

**Ethics.** Do not fake duration. If it takes 8s, do not cut it to look like 1s without saying so.

**Failures.** "Lightning fast" type over a normal spinner.

## social-proof

**Psychological goal.** Borrow certainty from real others.

**Works when.** You have real quotes, ratings, press, or usage the user supplied or the store listing shows.

**Does not work.** No proof. Then pick another strategy.

**Hooks.** One specific true line, huge, then the product that earned it.

**Visual language.** Review as typography, not a fake card stack. If you show stars, they are real.

**Pacing.** Proof → product, or product → proof as button.

**Copy.** Verbatim. Attribution if you have it.

**Ideal for.** Products with genuine reviews or named customers who consented.

**Ethics.** See `claims.md`. No invented ReviewCards. No implied celebrity.

**Failures.** Avatar walls, "trusted by 10,000+", logo salad without rights.

## objection-killer

**Psychological goal.** Remove the one reason they will not try.

**Works when.** Research or the repo makes the objection obvious: price, account, cloud, learning curve, privacy, "I already have X".

**Does not work.** When you cannot actually answer it.

**Hooks.** The objection, spoken in their words, then visually contradicted.

**Visual language.** Split or match-cut: feared thing vs actual thing (sign-up wall vs empty home; $9/mo vs one-time).

**Pacing.** State → kill → demo.

**Copy.** Plain. "No account." only if true.

**Ideal for.** Privacy apps, paid-once tools, replacements for bloated incumbents.

**Ethics.** The kill must be true in the build you are advertising.

**Failures.** Answering a weak objection. "It's easy!" with no picture of ease.

## relatable

**Psychological goal.** Recognition: "that is my Tuesday".

**Works when.** There is a specific scene (parent at 22:40, student in a library photo dump, founder with 47 tabs).

**Does not work.** Broad "busy professionals".

**Hooks.** The scene, too close. No product yet.

**Visual language.** Lived-in UI: notifications, messy camera rolls, half-filled forms. Then the product as relief.

**Pacing.** Linger on recognition, snap to help.

**Copy.** Their sentence. Slang if it is really theirs.

**Ideal for.** Consumer apps with a sharp persona.

**Ethics.** Do not stereotype cruelly. Do not use a real person's data.

**Failures.** Stock "woman smiling at laptop".

## story

**Psychological goal.** Feel a beat, then meet the tool that belongs in it.

**Works when.** 15 seconds can hold a tiny arc (lost → found, shame → keep, noise → one photo).

**Does not work.** Feature-led enterprise with no human beat.

**Hooks.** Character-adjacent object (a camera roll, a draft, a ticket).

**Visual language.** Continuity. One location in z-space. Cuts are emotional, not informational.

**Pacing.** 3 acts compressed: sit, turn, land.

**Copy.** Dialogue-like, very little.

**Ideal for.** Creative, memory, wellness, consumer narrative products.

**Ethics.** Do not tell a trauma story you cannot honor. No fake names as testimonials.

**Failures.** Mini-movie that never shows the product. Or product taped onto a story at the end.

---

## challenge

**Psychological goal.** Invite them into a rule they half-want to obey.

**Works when.** The product *is* a constraint (12 shots, one task, 25 minutes, no likes).

**Does not work.** Unconstrained productivity suites.

**Hooks.** The rule, stated as a dare. A visible quota.

**Visual language.** Scoreboard, remaining count, a tight frame that feels like a ring.

**Pacing.** Progressive. Each shot raises the stakes of the rule.

**Copy.** Imperative, short.

**Ideal for.** Habit, camera, focus, fitness, games.

**Ethics.** Do not challenge people into harm. No disordered-eating or grinding dares.

**Failures.** "Are you ready?" with no rule. Gamification stickers.

---

## feature-wow

**Psychological goal.** One interaction should make them lean in.

**Works when.** There is a visually shocking moment (a grade landing on a photo, a layout exploding into order, live capture).

**Does not work.** Hidden backend magic with nothing to film.

**Hooks.** Mid-wow. Do not waste the first second on the brand.

**Visual language.** Macro → the effect → pull out to reveal it lives in this app.

**Pacing.** Build, hit, breathe.

**Copy.** Name the action, not the feature name from the settings screen.

**Ideal for.** Creative tools, novel gestures, spatial/AR, unique editors.

**Ethics.** Real effect, real cost (time, quality). No staged "AI magic" that the build cannot do.

**Failures.** Every feature gets a wow. Then none do.

---

## premium

**Psychological goal.** This is for people with taste. Including the viewer.

**Works when.** Materials, type, motion, and price already say craft.

**Does not work.** Cheap-looking UI or a race-to-free product.

**Hooks.** Material close-up. Slow light. A mark of restraint (one word, huge).

**Visual language.** Negative space, long push-ins, controlled parallax, black/cream/metal — whatever the brand actually is.

**Pacing.** Slow. Cuts are decisions.

**Copy.** Few words. No exclamation marks.

**Ideal for.** High-design consumer, pro tools, paid-once craft apps.

**Ethics.** Do not imply luxury outcomes (wealth, status purchases) the product does not confer.

**Failures.** Gold gradients, serif-on-everything, "for those who know".

---

## chaos-calm

**Psychological goal.** Sell relief as a bodily feeling.

**Works when.** Input is visually chaotic and output is simple.

**Does not work.** Already-calm products (meditation with no mess to show).

**Hooks.** Overlapping UI, notifications, a noisy roll. Then a hard cut to one object.

**Visual language.** Handheld / layered / desynced → locked-off, spacious, single subject.

**Pacing.** Unstable to smooth. Audio follows if present.

**Copy.** None until calm. Then one line.

**Ideal for.** Inbox, photos, notes, home screens, focus.

**Ethics.** Do not pathologize normal life. The calm must be the product, not a stock spa.

**Failures.** Shake filter + zen quote. Calm footage with no product.

---

## loss-aversion

**Psychological goal.** Make the status quo feel like a leak.

**Works when.** They are already losing something the product keeps (photos never seen, time in meetings, money in unused subscriptions).

**Does not work.** Brand-new desires with no current loss.

**Hooks.** A number going the wrong way. A pile they will never open.

**Visual language.** Accumulation, burial, overflow. Then retrieval of one precious thing.

**Pacing.** Weight, then a lift.

**Copy.** Concrete loss. No "don't lose out" fluff.

**Ideal for.** Backup, memory, finance (no false savings), time trackers with real data.

**Ethics.** No fearmongering, no fake data-loss events, no "hackers are coming" unless that is the product and the claim is supportable.

**Failures.** Skull icon, red graphs you invented.

---

## identity

**Psychological goal.** "People like me use this."

**Works when.** The product encodes a tribe or a stance (film photographers, local-first nerds, parents who refuse cloud baby photos).

**Does not work.** Mass-market tools with no culture.

**Hooks.** In-group object or sentence. If you get it, you stay.

**Visual language.** Tribal material (not costume). Tools of the trade, not stock diversity shots.

**Pacing.** Confident, unhurried. You are not explaining the joke.

**Copy.** In-group language, still intelligible.

**Ideal for.** Niche creative, privacy diehards, crafts, scenes.

**Ethics.** Do not punch down. Do not fake community size.

**Failures.** "For the ones who get it" on a generic to-do app.
